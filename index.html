What goes wrong

Three failures, and none of them look like failures

These are not hard documents or bad scans. Each one produces a confident, plausible, wrong number that survives every review short of re-reading the lease.

01

The lease contradicts itself

Two clauses state different rents, usually a figure copied from the wrong row of a rent schedule. Each reads perfectly clearly on its own.

02

The unit is never stated

A rate quoted per square foot, with no period given. Per month and per year are twelve times apart, and both readings are grammatical.

03

The value is not in the document

A commencement date defined as “the date in the Delivery Notice”, and the notice was not in the file. Any date shown here was invented.

THE DIFFERENCE A CITATION MAKES

When every value carries its source, a disagreement takes ten seconds to settle instead of a week. That is the whole proposition.

Worked example

An office lease, $226,849 apart from itself

2.1 ... Base Rent ... in the amount of Twenty-Six Dollars and Fifty Cents ($26.50) per rentable square foot per annum. 2.2 ... The monthly installment of Base Rent during the first Lease Year shall be Forty-Four Thousand Three Hundred Sixty-Six and 95/100 Dollars ($44,366.95). 2.3 ... Base Rent shall increase by three percent (3.0%) on each anniversary ...
Derived
18,940 sf × $26.50 ÷ 12 = $41,825.83 a month
Stated
Clause 2.2 says $44,366.95 a month
Gap
$2,541.12 a month · $226,849 over the 84-month term
Diagnosis
$44,366.95 implies $28.11/sf, exactly the Year 3 rate under the 3% escalation. Clause 2.2 carries the wrong row of the schedule.
