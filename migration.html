Why lease data is the part that fails quietly

Mapping is not checking
A migration proves that a value moved from one field to another. It proves nothing whatsoever about whether the value was ever right.
The abstractor is gone
The person who entered the original record has moved on, and in most systems nothing records which clause each value came from or who decided the ambiguous ones. There is no trail to re-walk and no way to sample the work.
The consequence lands later
Lease data drives the ASC 842, GASB 87 or SFFAS 54 ledger. A wrong commencement date moves every payment in the schedule and changes the term used to discount the liability. It does not announce itself at cutover; it announces itself when an auditor samples it.
Nobody owns it
The integrator owns the platform. The client owns the accounting. The lease documents themselves sit between the two, and on most projects the documents are the only artefact nobody is contracted to read.

THE WINDOW ARGUMENT

A migration is the one moment an organisation can justify re-reading its leases: budget approved, attention already on the portfolio, and a date in the calendar. After cutover that window closes for a decade.
